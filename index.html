How to read this map

These four forms highlight different coordination logics, not a ranking. Stable work favors standards; dynamic work needs adaptation. Authority can sit with a chief or with experts. The three forms below describe divisions, shared mission, or contested power beyond these four fundamentals.

The design premise

Pure forms are caricatures. Real organizations hold a dominant force in check with another force, or combine different forms in different parts of the enterprise.

Seven forms

One framework.
Many structures.

Each form resolves coordination differently. The useful question is not “Which is best?” but “Which force does this work require, and what will keep that force from becoming pathological?”

A strong chief can unify action quickly; disciplined resource choices keep the enterprise from becoming purely personal.

How work is coordinated

Direct supervision. Most people look to one chief, while formal rules and planning systems stay thin.

Who holds power

Centralized around the chief; hierarchy is shallow and spans of control can be very wide.

Works when

New, small, founder-led, in crisis, or facing a simple environment where rapid focused action matters.

Typical risk

The organization becomes dependent on one person; succession, overreach, and lost nuance are persistent dangers.

Read it through

Start-up founder, corner diner, a new NGO, or a turnaround chief taking temporary command.

Remember this

The Personal Enterprise does not merely lack structure. It resists structure because structure competes with the chief’s authority.

Matching business model

What it offers

Speed, conviction, and a distinct point of view

How work is done

Direct decisions, deals, and hands-on coordination

How money flows

Often transactional; can be founder-funded

What stays inside

Mostly owned and tightly controlled

Likely model tension

Growth or recurring services expose the organization's dependence on one person.

Offer · Block 02

Value propositions

The block

The bundle of products and services that creates value for a specific customer segment.

Which customer problem are we solving—and why would this offer be chosen over an alternative?

Organization view

Novel or customized value often pulls work toward Project Pioneers; stable professional judgment can point toward a Professional Assembly.

A value proposition that sounds distinctive may still rely on an operating model the organization cannot support.

Read the system

The Canvas is not nine independent checklists. A change in customer segment, value proposition, revenue logic, or key activity can make a different organization form more coherent.

Business-model patterns

Patterns are systems,
not templates.

The book’s patterns show recurring ways to solve a business-model challenge. Each changes the economics, the boundary of the firm, and the organization required to make it work.

Pattern 01

Unbundled businesses

Relationship, innovation, and infrastructure work can conflict when they share one operating logic.

Before

One company combines customer relationships, product innovation, and infrastructure under one roof.

Challenge

Different economics and cultures create trade-offs, slower decisions, and diluted focus.

What the pattern does

Separate or protect the three business logics while coordinating their shared dependencies.

Organization view

Often a Divisional Form or assembled hybrid: relationship units, project units, and a shared infrastructure platform.

Book examples: Private banking and mobile telecommunications.

Model design

Make the model visible.
Then test the logic.

Business-model innovation can start from resources, an offer, customers, or economics. The Canvas becomes useful when teams use it to surface assumptions, compare alternatives, and prototype organizational consequences.

Multiple models

An established organization can hold more than one business model. When the models conflict, the design question is whether to integrate, separate, or phase that separation—considering strategic similarity, conflict severity, risk, and the synergies that must still be protected.

Cases

From a model pattern
to a real company.

One well-known company is selected for each pattern. Read the short passage, see how it maps to the organization, then use the official link for deeper context or reopen the pattern in Business → Org.

01 · Unbundling IBM → Kyndryl Infrastructure separated from cloud, consulting, and software.

Two different operating logics, allowed to separate

IBM and Kyndryl · 2021

IBM completed the spin-off of its managed infrastructure services business as Kyndryl. Kyndryl could focus on operating outsourced infrastructure at scale, while IBM positioned its remaining portfolio around hybrid cloud, software, and consulting. The separation made two different economic logics easier to manage independently.

Organization link: infrastructure resembles a Programmed core, while advisory and innovation work need Professional or Project freedom. The new company boundary becomes part of the organization design, and partnerships must recreate the coordination lost through separation.

Read IBM’s official case source ↗
02 · Long Tail Amazon A vast platform aggregates many small and niche offers.

One platform, an enormous catalog of niches

Amazon Marketplace · Global

Amazon Marketplace connects buyers with millions of independent sellers, whose products range from mainstream items to highly specific niches. Individual niche products may sell only occasionally, but together they create a vast assortment that can be discovered through Amazon’s search, reviews, payments, advertising, and logistics infrastructure.

Organization link: a Programmed platform core provides scale and repeatable rules, while independent sellers create Long Tail variety outside that core. The design tension is preserving niche diversity without letting standardized marketplace controls force every seller into the same model.

Read Amazon’s official case source ↗
03 · Multi-sided platform Apple App Store Users and developers create value through one governed platform.

A central platform orchestrates a vast developer network

Apple App Store · Global

The App Store connects people seeking software with developers who create it. Apple governs distribution, review, discovery, payments, and commercial terms, while developers supply much of the product variety and innovation. More useful applications can attract more users, which in turn makes the platform more valuable to developers.

Organization link: Apple’s platform stewardship is centrally coordinated, but the ecosystem is distributed. This is a hybrid of a controlled machine-like platform and a project-like developer network. The central tension is how much rule-setting and value capture can coexist with ecosystem autonomy.

Read Apple’s official platform source ↗
04 · FREE Google Search Free consumer access is funded by another side of the model.

Users search for free; advertisers fund the system

Google · Global

Google Search is available to users without a direct charge. Advertising creates the main cross-subsidy: businesses pay to reach search users through paid results and other advertising formats. The enormous user base, detailed intent signals, and machine-scale infrastructure make the advertising side valuable enough to sustain the free service.

Organization link: the free side grows through scale and automated service, while the paying side requires measurement, sales, and optimization. The organizational risk is letting advertising metrics dominate decisions in ways that weaken user trust or product quality.

Read Google’s official company source ↗
05 · Open model P&G Connect + Develop Internal capability meets a global network of external innovators.

Open innovation turns the company boundary into a resource

Procter & Gamble · Global

P&G’s Connect + Develop program invites outside innovators, suppliers, universities, and individuals to help solve product and technology challenges. P&G brings customer insight, brands, testing, manufacturing, and distribution to the collaboration, while external partners contribute ideas and specialist knowledge that may not exist inside the company.

Organization link: open value creation behaves more like a Project network and community than a closed hierarchy. The organization still needs strong gateways, intellectual-property rules, internal champions, and governance so that external ideas can move into commercialization without losing quality or control.

Read P&G’s official program page ↗

Change paths

Organizations do not stand still.

A form is a tendency, not a destiny. Age, success, crisis, external control, and political pressure can move an organization toward another form or hybrid.

Stage 01 / Birth

The personal beginning

New organizations usually turn toward a founding chief because resources, people, facilities, and culture all have to be assembled from scratch.

Personal Enterprise Direct supervision

What is happening

Direct supervision is the most natural first coordinating mechanism. Mutual adjustment is limited until people know one another, and standards take time to establish.

The design question: how does the founder create enough structure for others to act without turning the enterprise into a cult of personality?

Common pathways

Transitions are conditional.

The life cycle is a set of leading tendencies, not a sequence every organization must follow. Select a pathway to see the condition that makes it plausible and the tradeoff it introduces.

Most common pathway

Founder-led to machine-like

If the business matures into mass production or mass service, professional management can replace the founder and formalize work and outputs. The transition can be relatively smooth.

What makes it fit: stable, repetitive operating work and a need for coordination at scale.

Seek simplicity and distrust it.

Start with a useful form. Then look for the counterforce, the cross-cutting team, the protected pocket of difference, and the pathway people actually walk. That is where a living organization appears.
